(b) WHERE IT IS THEATRE — the four- and five-figure interconnect/speaker-cable tier: directional arrows on copper, cryogenic treatment, cable lifters, burn-in narratives; the measurement community's receipts (Audio Science Review's cable measurements, the classic blind-test literature incl. the famous coat-hanger test, ABX history) laid against the subjectivist press that reviews $10k cables without measurement. |Chinese woks|3|The single most-used pan in a Singapore kitchen, taken to its makers. The hierarchy from metal up: carbon steel as the default answer (gauge/weight trade-offs, hand-hammered vs spun vs stamped), cast iron's thin Chinese form (unlike Western cast iron), and why nonstick and most stainless miss the point of wok hei; round vs flat bottom for the reader's actual stove (induction/gas), handle forms (Cantonese ears vs northern stick), and seasoning as the ritual that IS the product — the blue-steel burn-in, the patina economics, why a $40 wok outcooks a $400 pan once seasoned. THE MAKERS: the Cantonese hand-hammering bench — Chan Chi Kee (Hong Kong, Shanghai Street institution) and the surviving HK/Guangzhou smiths; Shanghai Street's shops as a living atelier crawl; the famous Zhangqiu iron woks of Shandong (the 36,000-hammer-blows story told HONESTLY — the documentary fame, the counterfeit flood that followed, what's verifiable now); Japan's carbon-steel bench (Yamada Kogyosho hand-hammered, River Light); the Western translators (Cen brothers' Wok Shop San Francisco, Made In/Smithey honestly assessed as latecomers). White-label truths: who actually spins the pans behind hip DTC brands. Communities: r/carbonsteel, r/wok, Serious Eats' wok canon (Kenji López-Alt's The Wok as the reference text), Chinese Cooking Demystified's maker interviews, Woks of Life. Prices tiered honestly: entry-of-quality ~S$30-60 (a spun carbon-steel from a Chinatown dry-goods shop is genuinely excellent), sweet spot ~S$80-150 (hand-hammered CCK/Yamada), 'grail' hand-hammered Zhangqiu/artisan pieces — with the sceptic asking hard whether the grail tier cooks better or just tells a better story. SINGAPORE ANGLE, strong: the Chinatown kitchenware shops (Sia Huat on Temple Street as the trade's open secret, Ngai Heng and the dry-goods stalwarts), local stove reality (HDB induction vs gas and what that does to the round-bottom question), and the hawker-wok pedigree as living proof of the buy-once case. Care/longevity: rust panic vs reality, re-seasoning, the decades-old family wok as the desk's ethos made iron.|
|Watch winders|4|The reader's own drawer (he collects mechanical watches), and a category split cleanly between genuine engineering and velvet-lined theatre — a natural sequel to the cable special's honesty test, but with a real buy-once answer at the end. THE ENGINEERING TIER: what a winder actually must do (TPD ranges per calibre, bidirectional programs, sleep phases, magnetism avoided — ISO shielding claims tested), and the makers who build like instrument-makers: Orbita (US, the tourbillon-of-winders reputation and its rotorwind 'free ride' mechanism), Bernard Favre (Swiss, the planet double-axis as genuine mechanism), Elma/ Erwin Sattler (German instrument pedigree), RAPPORT London (the heritage bench), and the honest mid-tier that owns the sweet spot — Wolf (patented count-verified TPD, the one marketing claim that is actually a mechanism; assess honestly), Barrington, and the respectable Asian OEM tier (Klarstein/cheaper Amazon spinners) named for what they are. White-label truths: how many 'luxury' winder brands share the same Chinese motor assemblies — the Mabuchi-motor question; what a Swiss price buys beyond the box. THE SCEPTIC, load-bearing and evenhanded: the horological community's real split — watchmakers (incl. voices the Rabbit Hole's watchmaking desk will later cite) who argue most watches DON'T need winding (wear rotation, full wind-down between wears is fine or even preferable, service intervals not extended by winding), the counter-case where winders genuinely earn their keep (perpetual calendars, annual calendars, the grail-complication owner's setting cost), and the 'a winder is furniture jewellery' read given its steel-man. Communities: WatchUSeek's endless winder wars, r/Watches, Omega/Rolex forum threads, Hodinkee/Fratello coverage, watchmaker YouTube (the service-interval argument). Prices tiered: the ~S$100 spinner (what breaks, what magnetizes), the S$400-900 Wolf/Barrington sweet spot, the four-figure Orbita/Favre tier — with the what-you're-paying-for test explicit at each step. SG angle: local stockists (Sincere/Hour Glass boutique markups vs direct), humidity and cabinet storage honestly, and whether the reader's actual rotation (a Grand Seiko and friends) needs one at all — the desk should have the courage to conclude 'maybe none' if that's where the evidence lands.|
