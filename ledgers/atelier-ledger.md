|Ski gadgets &amp; on-mountain electronics|78|The desk's hardest case — buy-once meets a battery; sorted into durable electronics / scheduled-replacement / rental / theatre. |Claw machines (crane / UFO-catcher cabinets)|79|The one arcade machine everyone calls rigged — and both halves are true; fairness is a number in a menu. MECHANISM: two/three-motor gantry + cable-drum claw; Western payout-percentage model (full grip ~1 play in 18, Smithsonian "Captain Claw" manual default) vs Japanese UFO Catcher skill-tuning (two-button single-axis, 橋渡し hashiwatashi "bridge" settings), but kakuritsu-ki probability machines exist there too — fairness is a setting, not a property. MAKERS (corrections): Sega UFO Catcher (since 1985, pillarless 9 current), Bandai Namco Clena (NOT Taito), Elaut (Belgium 1959, first 3-motor single cart), Smart Industries (Iowa, cranes since 1985 — NOT the 1920s, no verified "Big Choice/Cotton Candy"). WHITE-LABEL: desktop-claw flood = one Guangdong OEM chassis under a dozen badges. PRICES: mini-claw ~S$100 novelty; reconditioned Sega ~£2,700/~S$4,600 (Liberty Games); new Western commercial from ~US$4,350 (BHM); grey-import JP ¥150-400k held. HOME: 100V step-down (not adapter, check Hz), ~900kg Elaut lift, 20-yr cabinet w/ PCB+coin-mech failure points → run free-play. SG: Gambling Control Act 2022 swept claw machines in, S$100 prize cap + cash-payout ban from 1 Mar 2024, GRA/SPF licensed. Sceptic: cost-per-win ~$20-40/plush; Japan's ~¥800 cap made the product BETTER; the only claw you can trust is one whose dip switches are yours. Next on the Bench: Umbrellas, Kitchen knives, Leather bags.|
